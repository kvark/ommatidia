# DLSS 4/5 reconstruction probes — 2026-09-04

## Question

Which public lessons from NVIDIA's DLSS 4 reconstruction report and DLSS 5
generative-rendering report transfer to a small, portable physical
reconstructor without copying a product name or assuming NVIDIA-only kernels?
The source reading and boundary between the two tasks are recorded in
[`dlss-4-5-lessons.md`](../dlss-4-5-lessons.md). This note records the
controlled experiments.

## More spatial range is not the missing evidence

The first probe added a fourth U-Net level to the balanced B8 low-resolution
correction model. This is the cheapest existing-operation approximation of
DLSS 4's longer-range aggregation: no attention primitive, Meganeura operation,
or shader was added. Both models used the same procedural/ABO bytes, batch,
seed, 1,500-step cosine schedule, 16-frame phase/lobe history, and 480-crop ABO
selector.

| model | parameters | estimated 1080p arithmetic | PSNR | SSIM | LF PSNR | relMSE | detail | temporal | LF temporal |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| existing 3-level B8 | 78,272 | 17.5 GFLOP | **33.34 dB** | **0.9386** | **37.63 dB** | **0.02047** | 86% | — | — |
| 4-level B8 | 296,064 | 21.2 GFLOP | 33.15 dB | 0.9259 | 37.41 dB | 0.02067 | **88%** | +0.28 dB | +0.64 dB |

The added level consumes 3.8 times the parameters and 21% more arithmetic yet
loses 0.19 dB, 0.0127 SSIM, and 0.22 dB of broad-illumination fidelity. More
spatial reach alone is rejected. DLSS 4's result is evidence for long-range
**spatiotemporal sample aggregation**, not evidence that making this spatial
U-Net deeper will uncover information absent from its input.

## A low-frequency label is not a tone branch

DLSS 5 separates low-frequency tone behavior from high-frequency structure.
The first low-cost translation box-filtered the existing RGB correction target
during training while retaining the exact split-radiance base at inference.
Four- and eight-pixel blocks were tested and the implementation was removed
after the gate.

At the structure-safe correction share, the 4x4 label reached 33.00 dB /
0.9509 SSIM / 37.24 dB LF PSNR on the 480 ABO crops. The ordinary label reached
33.12 / 0.9513 / 37.35 and also won relative, worst-crop, and temporal error.
On 240 furnished-interior crops the filtered label gained only 0.0002 SSIM,
while the ordinary label won PSNR, LF PSNR, relative error, worst-crop error,
and temporal stability. The 8x8 arm was worse again. Tone/structure separation
remains a useful design concept, but erasing frequencies from the label is not
a useful implementation of it.

## Exact motion for moving, jittered reconstruction

Projection jitter and scene motion previously could not be combined in the
generator. One input-resolution vector had to be shared by all four output
sub-pixels, which is wrong where a reconstruction footprint spans moving
foreground and background. The data, CPU teacher/evaluator, shared-context GPU
runtime, and Blade helper now accept an optional output-resolution motion plane
in output-pixel units. Old datasets and hosts continue to use input-resolution
motion in input-pixel units. No network operation or shader group was added;
the existing unpack pass reads one optional texture.

The first capture found a second assumption: phase/lobe samples were stored at
their old screen texel. They are now treated as one sparse output-resolution
image and reprojected across both low texels and phase slots with the exact
motion and output surfaces. A capture that asks for scene motion plus
projection jitter is rejected unless it also requests the HR G-buffer, so the
generator cannot silently create the ambiguous old representation.

A GPU recurrence test deliberately assigns different vectors to adjacent
output sub-pixels and agrees with the CPU reconstruction, including reset. A
real Blade capture combines 16 projection-jittered frames, curved camera
motion, independently moving objects, shadows, texture, gloss, split radiance,
and exact output surfaces. New exact-motion datasets can be balanced with old
ones because motion is temporal evidence rather than a conditioning-plane
requirement.

Before retraining, the frozen balanced B8 checkpoint at its selected 20%
correction was scored on nine unseen moving sequences (540 crops, 1,024-spp
references):

| reconstruction | PSNR | SSIM | LF PSNR | relMSE | worst crop | detail | energy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| split guide | 29.54 dB | 0.8620 | 34.74 dB | 0.10028 | 5.22 | 53% | 0.995 |
| frozen B8, 20% | **29.75 dB** | **0.8632** | **35.07 dB** | **0.08860** | **4.43** | **54%** | **0.995** |

The frozen model generalizes and improves every error/stability metric, but its
moving-sequence gain (+0.21 dB, +0.0012 SSIM, +0.33 dB LF) is still too small
to explain away the visual gap. Reprojecting phase history also removes the
checkpoint's apparent 0.4% energy loss and improves its worst crop by another
12%; the old screen-attached history had been the cause, not learned exposure.
The new linear-radiance energy score makes that distinction observable instead
of hiding it in a compressed-space average.

Adding the 16 new moving sequences as a third equally sampled training corpus
did not improve this constrained head. At full correction it reached 30.01 dB
and 35.51 dB LF, but lost SSIM (0.8566) and overshot energy (1.031). At the
same frozen 20% correction it scored 29.73 dB / 0.8632 SSIM / 35.06 dB LF,
slightly behind the old checkpoint on every error metric. The checkpoint is
rejected. Motion data cannot help a head whose only state is the already-fixed
low-resolution phase/lobe estimate enough to justify another model; the next
probe puts the motion into a recurrent output path.

## Reset behavior and the estimator's radiance space

The first previous-output retrain failed a deployment condition before it
tested architecture: the batcher sampled only frames with history. Its first
frame therefore came from a distribution the model never saw, and the error
was fed back. Previous-output training now includes frame zero at its natural
one-in-four frequency; the temporal target and history validity are zero for
that slot, and evaluation reports reset/cut quality and its deterministic base
separately.

That exposed a second, larger error. The kernel was described as a convex
combination of physical radiance, but it actually averaged
`compress(radiance)`. For the transform `x/(1+x)`, equal taps at 0 and 4
decompress to 0.667 instead of the physical mean 2.0. Wider denoising therefore
guaranteed darkening. A sidecar bit now gives new kernels linear taps and moves
compression after the average in the CPU teacher, training graph, and existing
unpack pass. Missing bits retain the old path for checkpoint compatibility.

Linear gathering alone raised mean energy from 0.735 to 0.867 at 2,000 steps,
but exposed fireflies and lost temporal quality. Mixing that physical gather
with the existing deterministic temporal HR guide is the compact viable arm:

| 2k arm, 384 new-motion crops | PSNR | SSIM | relMSE | detail | energy | LF PSNR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| HR guide | 25.77 dB | 0.7312 | 0.13465 | 36% | 0.994 | 30.98 dB |
| temporal guide | 25.48 dB | 0.7721 | 0.06186 | 40% | 0.948 | 28.94 dB |
| linear gather + guide mix | **25.77 dB** | 0.7610 | 0.06288 | **43%** | 0.933 | **29.48 dB** |

The guide mix adds four head channels, no model block, and no new operation or
shader group. A kernel-specific low-frequency loss and a smaller temporal-loss
weight were both worse and were removed. With this one-step teacher, the
explicit temporal loss selected a stable dark answer; it should return only
with rollout training and reactive evidence rather than another weight sweep.

The selected mixed-corpus arm trained for 8,000 steps. Before repairing the
capture issue below, it reached 25.96 dB / 0.7537 SSIM / 30.21 dB LF PSNR,
0.954 energy, and 45% detail on 384 held-out crops: +0.48 dB per-frame and
+0.45 dB low-frequency temporal over the guide. Its reset frame gains 1.45 dB
and 2.47 dB LF. The untouched older audit gains 0.24 dB and 0.67 dB LF
temporal, so the improvement is not isolated to the selector. Fine-grained
temporal error is still 0.38 dB worse than the guide and remains a release
blocker.

## Multi-sample capture was erasing camera motion

Blade correctly keeps current and previous cameras in its G-buffer contract,
but the data harness prepared the same current camera for every accumulated
reference path and ReSTIR settling iteration, then read the G-buffer only at
the end. Both camera slots then held the current frame. On an identical
two-frame path probe, old output-resolution motion peaked at 0.000021 pixels;
snapshotting the primary surfaces after the first iteration produces 0.729
pixels. Radiance/reservoir processing continues unchanged. The generator now
asserts nonzero LR camera motion as well, and LavaPipe covers the ReSTIR case.

The 256-sequence train and 32-sequence holdout were regenerated from their
existing high-sample targets. A byte audit found every LR input, target,
depth, normal, and material byte identical; only 243,346,896 of 268,435,456
training HR-motion bytes changed. On the corrected holdout, the frozen 8k
model moves from 25.96 to 25.98 dB and its worst relative-error crop improves
from 1.25 to 0.31. Valid previous-output coverage rises from 95.9% to 97.7%.
This is primarily a correctness fix, not a hidden quality windfall. The file
format records motion resolution and units but cannot prove when a renderer
sampled its camera history, so moving HR-G-buffer captures made with the old
harness must be regenerated rather than relabelled.

Retraining on the repaired records reaches 25.84 dB / 0.7578 SSIM / 29.68 dB
LF PSNR, or +0.36 dB over the temporal guide. Exact motion reduces the
fine-grained temporal deficit from 0.38 to 0.17 dB and improves broad temporal
error by 0.58 dB. On the untouched audit, fine temporal error is effectively
level (-0.01 dB) and broad temporal error improves by 1.27 dB. Mean energy is
still only 0.933 and tight glossy structure is visibly smoothed, so the
corrected run is the honest baseline rather than a quality endpoint.
Closing only the previous-output gate changes spatial PSNR from 25.84 to 25.79
dB, but changes fine and broad temporal comparisons from -0.12/+0.61 dB to
-1.63/-1.64 dB on full frames. The small recurrent share is providing temporal
coherence rather than merely improving a static score.

Post-training history calibration exposes the DLSS 4 stability/responsiveness
tradeoff rather than one universal optimum. Halving the raw history mixture
reaches 25.85 dB but loses 0.62 dB fine and 0.15 dB broad temporal quality to
the guide. A 1.5x mixture reaches 25.79 dB while beating the guide by 0.24 and
1.20 dB respectively. The trained 1.0 setting remains the balanced default;
the stronger preset should become an application control only after reactive
evidence makes lag/ghosting measurable in clips.

## Noise floor and more-ray control

An independently rendered 1,024-spp path range for the first eight holdout
sequences agrees in mean energy to 0.001%. Pairwise PSNR is 34.72 dB, implying
a 37.74 dB one-reference noise floor, and block-averaged PSNR is 58.46 dB.
However, independent-reference SSIM is only 0.852 and measured high-frequency
detail differs by 33%. Fine reference grain must therefore not be interpreted
as structure that a denoiser ought to reproduce; the model's much larger
low-frequency error is not explained by label noise.

Keeping the same model and scenes but averaging four independent input paths
raises PSNR from 25.84 to 28.23 dB and LF PSNR from 29.68 to 35.29 dB. The
four-path temporal guide itself reaches 28.15 dB. This is a ray-count control,
not yet a matched-time claim, but it establishes that missing Monte Carlo
evidence is a major current ceiling and that future model comparisons need the
same total-frame-time baseline used in the DLSS 5 report.

Two tempting fixes fail controlled gates. Removing the deterministic guide and
training the linear gather for the full 8,000 steps reaches only 23.28 dB /
0.6427 SSIM, 0.886 energy, and loses 1.88 dB temporal stability to the HR
guide. Conversely, resuming the mixed model for 1,000 steps with an auxiliary
crop-mean loss moves energy only from 0.933 to 0.937, loses PSNR and SSIM, and
turns a 0.58 dB broad-temporal win into a 0.03 dB loss. The experimental loss
was removed. Energy needs a better estimator/state objective, not another
scalar penalty on one-step crops.

Width does not change that conclusion. At the same 4,000-step schedule, b16
scores 25.75 dB / 0.7583 SSIM and b24 scores 25.78 / 0.7612. The 0.03 dB and
0.0029 SSIM gains cost 692,388 rather than 319,068 parameters and 148.6 rather
than 77.3 GFLOP per 1080p frame; b24 also loses more fine and broad temporal
quality to the guide. The 88,340-parameter b8 arm reaches 25.80 dB, but learns
only a 2.2% valid history share and loses 0.86 dB fine / 0.67 dB broad temporal
quality to the guide. B16 is the smallest tested width that learns useful
recurrence; b24 is not a viable quality/latency trade.

Phase-resolved history is a closer gate. After 4,000 steps plus a 2,000-step
low-rate continuation it reaches 25.82 dB / 0.7611 SSIM / 0.943 energy versus
25.84 / 0.7578 / 0.933 for the 8k variance model. Relative error improves,
while LF PSNR loses 0.12 dB and fine/broad temporal comparisons lose 0.05/0.01
dB. It adds only 2.3 GFLOP, but the present native pack deliberately supports
basic/variance state only. This Pareto tie is not evidence to expand the
runtime yet; phase state should return inside the rollout-trained state gate,
where its deployment distribution can actually be tested.

## Real-mesh generalization remains open

Eight untouched Amazon-Berkeley object families were captured as moving,
jittered four-frame sequences with exact surfaces/motion and 1,024-spp targets.
The b16 baseline reaches 33.05 dB / 0.9322 SSIM / 0.995 energy, but the temporal
guide reaches 33.58 / 0.9435 / 0.998. The model loses 0.53 dB spatially and
0.38 dB fine temporal quality; even its reset frame is 0.59 dB behind. High
absolute PSNR on this easier distribution must not hide that regression.

A disjoint 24-family training capture tested whether this was only missing
content diversity. A 2,000-step low-rate continuation balanced that corpus
equally with both procedural corpora. It scores 33.03 dB on the real holdout
and worsens fine temporal error to -0.72 dB. The original procedural holdout
stays near 25.82 dB but its temporal comparison falls from -0.17/+0.58 dB to
-0.45/+0.27 dB. This adaptation is rejected. Real geometry belongs in the
from-scratch rollout corpus, but data-only fine-tuning does not repair the
one-step recurrent objective.

A post-hoc confidence diagnostic makes the failure more specific. Multiplying
the learned physical-gather **odds** by 0.25 reduces its mean share from 20.6%
to 6.4%. On the eight-family full-frame run, that changes the network from
33.22 dB and 0.9334 SSIM to 33.74 dB and 0.9430, just above the temporal
guide's 33.58 dB and nearly level on SSIM. It also changes the fine/broad
temporal comparisons from -0.32/-0.01 dB to +0.59/+1.35 dB. On 32 procedural
full-frame crops it retains only a 0.01 dB spatial gain over the guide, but
improves fine/broad temporal error by 0.54/+1.11 dB. This is evidence that the
spatial gather is over-confident out of distribution and that recurrence is
repairing rather than causing the blotches. A 0.1 scale favors real-scene
stability (33.70 dB / 0.9437 SSIM) but falls to 25.86 dB on the procedural
frames; 0.5 retains 26.17 dB procedurally but lowers the real result to 33.66 /
0.9405. No global calibration dominates both distributions. It is not a
selected checkpoint result: the real set was inspected to choose the
multiplier and is no longer an untouched audit. The evaluator retains
`--guide-scale` for controlled calibration sweeps; rollout training must learn
this confidence, and a fresh mesh-family split must verify it.

At 960x540 to 1920x1080 on the RX 7900 XT, this experimental b16/r3 path is
15.77 ms median and 16.05 ms p90 for pack, model, unpack, and submissions. The
isolated model is 11.70 ms; convolution accounts for 65.6%, pointwise work
23.4%, normalization 8.3%, and data movement 2.7%. Timestamp instrumentation
costs 1.3% and covers 93.8% of profiled wall time, so these family totals are a
useful diagnosis rather than the earlier query-dominated trace. The 142.4 MiB
history allocation and optional HR primary pass remain outside the model-only
number.

## Decision

Retain exact motion, parallel batch target preparation, base reuse in
evaluation, and the energy diagnostic. Reject the deeper spatial model and the
band-limited label. The next architecture gate should spend capacity on causal,
bounded recurrent state and train it on moving/disoccluding evidence; only then
compare gated convolution with coarse current-query/history-key attention at
matched frame time. A large spatial transformer or iterative diffusion model
does not pass the evidence or portability gate.
